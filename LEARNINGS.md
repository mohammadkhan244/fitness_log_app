# Fitness 2.0 — Training Learnings

Durable training conclusions and classification decisions. This is the source of truth for any "basis" claim made in capabilityConfig.ts or elsewhere in the app. Update this file, not a paraphrase of it, when a new conclusion is confirmed.

- Rubber band theory confirmed in practice: slow tempo work at submaximal load builds a tendon foundation that makes fast tempo at higher loads feel manageable — observed directly with lat pulldown → fast tempo transfer.
- Effective tension over external load: slow tempo eliminates elastic/momentum assistance, potentially raising effective tendon tension even at submaximal external load — logged as a mechanistic research note, not yet directly tested.
- Iso-holds ≠ slow tempo: iso-holds cover fixed-angle isometric stiffness; slow tempo covers full-ROM eccentric loading — both are needed for complete coverage.
- Fatigue vs. stimulus: fatigue is a subjective signal, not equivalent to effective training stimulus.
- AMRAP-to-failure across consecutive sets: documented drop-off pattern — avoid.
- EMOM structure: rest intervals are the functional point — start KB hotel EMOMs at 6/5/4 reps (not 8/6/5) to achieve 17–34 second rest windows; this prevents the session from becoming a cardio circuit.
- Handstand ceiling: lats (not shoulders or pressing strength) are the current HSPU limiter — cable rows and inverted rows directly feed HSPU capacity.
- Deadlift: anchored as a permanent fixture in every gym session.
- Jefferson curl: a permanent program tool, not just recovery — it covers the posterior chain at an end-range lengthened position that cable rows do not; together they span the full posterior chain tension spectrum.
- Duck walking: reclassified as slow-tempo lower-body movement requiring 72-hour separation from fast-tempo lower work, not Guardian filler.
- Shadow boxing: identified as the driver of rotational/twisting back soreness — flagged for isolation and moderation.
- Handwritten notes serve internalization; AI logging serves raw data capture — do not retype handwritten reflections into the app.
- Overcoming isometrics (max/near-max effort against an immovable resistance, 1-10s holds) are a distinct stimulus from the yielding isometrics already logged (wall sits, hollow holds, handstand holds): overcoming isometrics train motor-unit recruitment and neural drive, and strength gained is joint-angle specific — transfers roughly 10-20° either side of the trained angle. Tracked separately with a required Joint Angle field so evidence can be matched to a capability's relevant range, not just a duration. Sources: themovementsystem.com/blog/overcoming-isometrics; thejacksonclinics.com/isometric-training-pushing-yielding (practitioner sources, not peer-reviewed — tag accordingly if surfaced in the Science tab).
- PAILs/RAILs (progressive/regressive angular isometric loading) trains end-range strength directly: PAILs is an overcoming isometric pushed into the end of current range (~10s, near-max effort), RAILs immediately follows as an active contraction pulling deeper into that range. Logged under Overcoming Isometric with Joint Angle set to the trained end-range angle. This is the first builtBy training input for wrist-extension and ankle-dorsiflexion — previously those capabilities had demonstratedBy evidence only, with nothing feeding them. Source: practitioner/FRC framework, not peer-reviewed (anaritaalmeida.substack.com/p/understanding-frc-and-how-it-is-a).
